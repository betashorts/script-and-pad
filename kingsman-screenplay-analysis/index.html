What Makes the Kingsman Screenplay Stand Out?

February 07, 2025

Introduction

Spy movies have followed a familiar formula for decades—until **Kingsman: The Secret Service** shook up the genre with its bold, stylish, and subversive screenplay. Co-written by **Jane Goldman** and **Matthew Vaughn**, the script merges classic espionage tropes with hyper-stylized action, unexpected humor, and character-driven storytelling. But what exactly makes the Kingsman screenplay stand out?

1. Jane Goldman’s Unique Screenwriting Approach

Jane Goldman, known for her **bold storytelling and sharp wit**, plays a crucial role in Kingsman’s distinct screenplay style. Having also co-written **Kick-Ass, X-Men: First Class, and Stardust**, Goldman’s writing stands out for its **mix of humor, emotional depth, and inventive world-building**.

Goldman’s Signature Screenwriting Traits:

  • Sharp, fast-paced dialogue that never overstays its welcome.
  • Well-developed characters—even side characters have personality and depth.
  • Stylized action sequences that are meticulously scripted, ensuring a visually stunning film.

2. Action Sequences That Feel Like Dance Choreography

One of the most talked-about aspects of **Kingsman** is its **fluid, high-energy action sequences**. The script doesn’t just describe fights—it meticulously choreographs them, ensuring they’re **integral to character and story**.

Example: The Church Scene


HARRY moves through the crowd with precision—an elegant brutality.
Heads slam, bodies fly. A symphony of violence set to Free Bird.

The action **feels like a well-rehearsed ballet**, which is rare in conventional spy films.
