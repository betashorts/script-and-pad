How to Know If a Movie Had a Bad Script

February 07, 2025

Introduction

We’ve all been there—watching a movie that had potential, but something felt off. More often than not, **a bad script** is to blame. But how can you tell if a movie suffered from poor screenwriting? Whether you're an aspiring filmmaker, a critic, or just a curious moviegoer, here’s a breakdown of the biggest red flags that indicate a weak script.

5 Signs of a Bad Movie Script

1. Weak or Inconsistent Characters

Great movies have **well-developed, consistent characters** with clear motivations. A bad script often fails in this area, resulting in:

  • Flat, one-dimensional characters: They exist only to move the plot forward.
  • Unrealistic decisions: Characters act irrationally or contradict their established personalities.
  • Lack of character growth: The protagonist ends up in the same place emotionally and mentally as they started.

2. Clunky or Unrealistic Dialogue

Great screenplays have **dialogue that feels real and serves a purpose**. Bad scripts suffer from:

  • Exposition-heavy dialogue: Characters explain the plot unnaturally.
  • Overuse of clichés: Every line sounds recycled from another movie.
  • Unrealistic speech patterns: Conversations feel forced and robotic.

3. Major Plot Holes

A strong screenplay **builds a logical and coherent story**. If a movie has glaring plot holes, the script wasn’t properly refined. Signs of bad plotting include:

  • Unresolved storylines: Characters disappear, or subplots are left hanging.
  • Illogical solutions: The hero suddenly wins with no real struggle.
  • Inconsistent world-building: Rules set earlier in the movie are broken later.
