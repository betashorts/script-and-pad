<h2>5 Signs of a Bad Movie Script</h2>

<h3>1. Weak or Inconsistent Characters</h3>
<p>Great movies have **well-developed, consistent characters** with clear motivations. A bad script often fails in this area, resulting in:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Flat, one-dimensional characters:</strong> They exist only to move the plot forward.</li>
  <li><strong>Unrealistic decisions:</strong> Characters act irrationally or contradict their established personalities.</li>
  <li><strong>Lack of character growth:</strong> The protagonist ends up in the same place emotionally and mentally as they started.</li>
</ul>

---

<h3>2. Clunky or Unrealistic Dialogue</h3>
<p>Great screenplays have **dialogue that feels real and serves a purpose**. Bad scripts suffer from:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Exposition-heavy dialogue:</strong> Characters explain the plot unnaturally.</li>
  <li><strong>Overuse of clichés:</strong> Every line sounds recycled from another movie.</li>
  <li><strong>Unrealistic speech patterns:</strong> Conversations feel forced and robotic.</li>
</ul>

---

<h3>3. Major Plot Holes</h3>
<p>A strong screenplay **builds a logical and coherent story**. If a movie has glaring plot holes, the script wasn’t properly refined. Signs of bad plotting include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Unresolved storylines:</strong> Characters disappear, or subplots are left hanging.</li>
  <li><strong>Illogical solutions:</strong> The hero suddenly wins with no real struggle.</li>
  <li><strong>Inconsistent world-building:</strong> Rules set earlier in the movie are broken later.</li>
</ul>
